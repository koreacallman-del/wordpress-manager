## 3. Primary Sources

### WHO/FAO definition of probiotics
- Original 2001 FAO/WHO joint definition: **"Live micro-organisms which, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host."** (FAO/WHO Expert Consultation, October 2001; formalized in FAO/WHO 2002 "Guidelines for the Evaluation of Probiotics in Food.")
- Refined 2014 wording (ISAPP consensus, Hill et al., *Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology*): "live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host."
- Source: [The International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics consensus statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrgastro.2014.66) (Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology, 2014); [Criteria to Qualify Microorganisms as "Probiotic" in Foods and Dietary Supplements](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2020.01662/full) (Frontiers in Microbiology, 2020); FAO/WHO Codex documentation: https://www.fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius/sh-proxy/en/?lnk=1&url=https://workspace.fao.org/sites/codex/Meetings/CX-720-44/nf44_06_Add.1e.pdf

### CFU counts in common fermented foods
- **Kimchi**: well-fermented kimchi reported in the range of ~10 million to 10 billion CFU/gram, varying by fermentation length/ingredients.
- **Sauerkraut**: fresh, unpasteurized sauerkraut ~1-10 billion CFU/gram; lactic acid bacteria (LAB) counts reported in the literature ranging 10³-10⁸ CFU/g depending on study/stage of fermentation.
- **Kefir**: ~27.7 billion CFU per 1-cup serving in one comparative test (highest of the foods tested).
- **Yogurt**: ~3.6 billion CFU per 1-cup serving in the same comparative test.
- ⚠️ **[확인 필요 / needs verification before publishing]**: The kefir/yogurt CFU figures above trace to a magazine lab-test writeup (Clean Eating Magazine, testing store-bought brands), not a peer-reviewed paper — treat as an illustrative range, not a citable clinical figure. The kimchi/sauerkraut ranges are more consistent with peer-reviewed ranges but should be cross-checked against a specific PMC paper before quoting an exact number in the article (direct PMC/NCBI access was blocked from this session — see method note above).
- Candidate peer-reviewed sources to verify directly (URLs found, not yet fetched due to egress block):

### Health benefits with clinical evidence
- **Gut microbiome diversity / inflammation — Stanford (Sonnenburg lab) 2021 RCT**: 36 healthy adults, 10-week diet, fermented-food arm (yogurt, kefir, fermented cottage cheese, kimchi, other fermented vegetables, vegetable brine drinks, kombucha) vs. high-fiber arm. Fermented-food diet **increased gut microbial diversity** and **decreased levels of 19 inflammatory proteins**; the high-fiber diet did not reduce those same inflammatory markers and microbial diversity stayed flat. Published in *Cell*, Aug 5, 2021 (online July 12, 2021), DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2021.06.019.
  - Source: https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(21)00754-6 ; https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2021/07/fermented-food-diet-increases-microbiome-diversity-lowers-inflammation.html
- **Immunity / respiratory infections**: Systematic reviews and a Cochrane review find probiotic supplementation can reduce incidence/duration of upper respiratory tract infections via improved mucosal immune function (increased sIgA, IFN-γ), though effect sizes vary by strain and not all trials are significant.
  - Cochrane review: https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006895.pub4/full
  - Systematic review (older adults): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8228160/
  - Narrative review (mechanisms): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33081138/
  - ⚠️ Hedge required per seo-rules.md: evidence is strain-specific and mixed — do not claim "probiotic foods prevent colds" as a blanket statement.
- **Mental health / gut-brain axis (psychobiotics)**: 2025 review specifically on **fermented vegetables as a source of psychobiotics** — evaluates evidence for anxiety, depression, cognitive function benefits.
  - Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40402417/ ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12602-025-10592-5 (Probiotics and Antimicrobial Proteins, 2025)
  - Broader systematic review of probiotic/prebiotic/synbiotic/fermented-food trials in psychiatric disorders (major depression most-studied, 19 trials) — evidence strongest for depression, sparse elsewhere: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763424000290
  - ⚠️ Hedge required: evidence for anxiety/cognition specifically is preliminary; depression has the most trial support but still not conclusive/blanket.

### Bacillus subtilis fermented foods (Korean/Japanese differentiator)
- Bacillus subtilis is the primary fermenting organism in **cheonggukjang, doenjang, gochujang** (Korea) and **natto** (Japan) — a functionally different probiotic category from the Lactobacillus/lactic-acid-bacteria foods (kimchi, sauerkraut, yogurt, kefir).
- B. subtilis strains isolated from Korean traditional fermented foods have shown acid/bile tolerance, antimicrobial activity, and immune-gene enhancement in in-vivo (C. elegans) models — genuine "potential probiotic" research, though largely preclinical.
- B. subtilis CSY191 isolated from doenjang showed ~58.3% survival rate under simulated gastric conditions (pH 3.0, 3 hours) in one study.
- ⚠️ Note for writer: cheonggukjang's dominant microbes are sometimes reported as a broader Bacillus community (B. subtilis, B. thermoamylovorans, B. licheniformis depending on study/region) — do not overstate a single-species claim; flag as "[확인 필요]" if a specific CFU number for cheonggukjang/doenjang is needed and not yet found in facts.md.
- Sources: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11475840/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10569484/ (natto) ; https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/9/12/1805 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590157524003523

### Practical/consumer-facing facts
- **Pasteurization kill zone**: Lactobacillus strains begin dying rapidly around 115-140°F; above ~140°F, most common probiotic strains are neutralized. Canned/shelf-stable sauerkraut and kimchi are heat-processed and generally contain no live cultures; refrigerated-aisle products labeled "raw," "unpasteurized," or "contains live cultures" are the reliable indicator. (Source: consumer-health summaries citing food-science heat thresholds — verify against a food-science primary source before quoting an exact °F figure in the article; treat as directionally correct, not lab-verified by this research pass.)
- **Serving guidance**: No official RDA for probiotics/fermented foods exists. Commonly cited practical range: ~2-3 servings/day of fermented foods; probiotic yogurt with ≥1 billion CFU per 4-6 oz serving is a commonly cited minimum-effective marker. A key evidence-based caveat: studies suggest many commercial probiotic foods deliver **far below** clinical-trial CFU doses — people may need 2-25 servings/day of some products to match RCT-tested amounts. This is a good hedge/nuance point for an SEO article that wants to avoid overpromising.

## 5. Recommended H2 Structure

Per seo-rules.md: H1 = one title; H2/H3 = question-form or verb-form; first sentence under each heading = direct answer; first paragraph overall = the article's conclusion (no intro/greeting).
